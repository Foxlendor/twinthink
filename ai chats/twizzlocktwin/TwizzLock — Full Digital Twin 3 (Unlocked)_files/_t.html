How it works

1. InsertPartially emptied 2L bottle drops into the sleeve, cap left exposed.
2. PumpBottle slides as a piston against the base valve, inflating the bag.
3. CollapseBag pressure pushes the PET walls inward, shrinking headspace.
4. LockRetainer band cinches around the reduced profile and holds it.
5. PourCap stays fully usable; sleeve can stay on or come off.

Open engineering question

Unsolved trade-off
Agitating a carbonated liquid nucleates bubble formation and can knock CO₂ out of solution faster than calm liquid — it's the same reason shaking a soda bottle makes it foam over. That means a fast, forceful pump stroke on TwizzLock could locally accelerate fizz loss even while it's shrinking the headspace that's supposed to preserve it long-term. A slow, controlled stroke likely avoids most of that penalty, but nobody has tested where the break-even point actually is, or whether a mechanism redesign (side compression instead of an axial piston, a damped/slow-release pump, a soft internal baffle) could get the volume-reduction benefit without the agitation cost. This twin's simulation now approximates that penalty for fast strokes so the trade-off is visible, but the real numbers need physical testing.

Have data, a fix, or a better mechanism? Open an issue on the TwinThink repo — this is deliberately left as a problem for others to help solve, not a finished answer.
